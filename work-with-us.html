Work with us

A research partner communities trust

For over two decades, Tracking California has paired rigorous environmental health data and research with deep, lasting relationships in the communities most affected. Funders and partners come to us for both.

What sets us apart

Deep technical and research expertise and genuine community trust — two qualities that rarely coexist in the same organization.

State agencies and academic partners rely on our rigor and credibility. Tribal Nations, farmworker communities, and environmental justice organizations rely on our long-term commitment and our willingness to show up, stay, and work on what matters to them. They come to us for data and science, content expertise, and thought leadership — and they stay because we make visible what standard monitoring systems are not designed to detect: the conditions, exposures, and health outcomes that fall through the cracks.

Where we're focused

Over the next three years, these areas of our expertise hold the most promising opportunities to advance environmental health and reduce disparities.

Air quality

Community monitoring networks, PM2.5 and smoke exposure surveillance, and technical assistance for AB 617 communities.

Water quality

Drinking water surveillance, exposure assessment for PFAS and harmful algal blooms, and support for small and under-resourced water systems.

Extreme heat

Heat-related illness surveillance, analysis of impacts on vulnerable populations, and heat-action planning and resilience.

We also expand into health services research — building on our sickle cell disease work across other diseases and outcomes — and sustain active work in disease surveillance, environmental epidemiology, and emerging hazards such as lithium extraction.

Who we serve

We are a California organization. Our data infrastructure, agency relationships, and community partnerships are rooted here.

Our priority populations are our longest-standing partners: rural communities, Tribal Nations, farmworker communities, and environmental justice communities — and other populations bearing the greatest environmental health burden and least served by government and public health infrastructure.

Why partner with us

What funders and collaborators gain by working with Tracking California.

  • Low indirect cost rate. Our 16.2% IDC means more of every grant dollar goes directly to the work.
  • Two decades of infrastructure. Data systems, institutional knowledge, and methods built and maintained since the early 2000s.
  • Agency and academic credibility. We co-develop the state's environmental health tracking data and publish peer-reviewed research.
  • Community trust that lasts. Long-term, co-investigator relationships — not parachute engagements.
  • Prime or subcontractor. We can lead a project or strengthen a partner's proposal with our data and analysis.
  • Built for the long term. We invest in multi-year, mission-aligned funder relationships that compound over time.

What makes a good fit

We're selective by design, so that the work we take on is work we can do well. The strongest opportunities tend to:

Build on our strengths

Draw on existing relationships, data infrastructure, and expertise rather than starting from scratch.

Center data and research

Surveillance, analysis, and infrastructure — the work that reveals what standard systems miss.

Serve California communities

Especially the rural, Tribal, farmworker, and environmental justice communities we partner with.

Create lasting impact

A clear difference for communities, for knowledge, or for our own capacity — with a funder relationship worth investing in.
